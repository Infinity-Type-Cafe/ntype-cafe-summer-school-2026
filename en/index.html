The ∞-type Café Summer School 2026 will officially begin on July 2, 2026. This is the second edition of the summer school (First Summer School)!

What is type theory? Born in logic, it grew up in mathematics, blossomed in computer science, and bore fruit in programming languages. It spans several closely intertwined disciplines. In mathematics, it has emerged as a new foundation for the subject, alongside set theory and category theory. In computer science, the word “type” immediately brings to mind the many kinds of programming languages in which types help compilers or interpreters perform compile-time checks. The emergence of the Curry–Howard correspondence prompted us to reconsider types, and the propositions-as-types perspective gave rise to type-based interactive proof assistants. It has become possible to write the proof of a concrete problem as a program—but to write such a program, one must first learn and become comfortable with the language it uses. On this point, students of both mathematics and computer science face their own difficulties. Mathematics students may first need to overcome the hurdle of programming, while computer science students may find the underlying mathematical foundations equally challenging. After overcoming countless difficulties, you finally write a program that a proof assistant accepts—but have you ever asked yourself whether it really is a proof? Are the functions defined in it well-defined? Are the objects constructed in it reasonable? Are the techniques it uses valid? Questions like these will keep pushing you forward, farther and farther…

One day, a beginner may happen upon type theory and begin exploring it. Their path will surely be full of twists and turns, because that is how we began as well. Through this event, we hope to help more students who want to study type theory, or simply learn what types are about. In return, we will also make more friends. That is our simple and sincere vision.

To quote 狗先生, the owner of the Infinity Type Café group:

On a cold night, two children lit a campfire, and more and more lost children gathered around it. They felt immeasurably happy.
When day broke, the children gradually found their own paths and left one by one. But they would never forget the campfire that had once kept them warm.

Foundation Courses

We know that every participant may come to type theory with a different level of familiarity. Since this is also our first summer school, we have specially invited seven lecturers to provide a welcoming set of foundation courses. The courses below follow a conventional path for learning type theory while also covering the prerequisites for the talks at this summer school. They are listed in the order in which they will be taught.

We hope these courses will help every participant, whatever their current level, and we encourage everyone to study ahead of time. If you encounter questions along the way, save them and ask the lecturer directly once the relevant course begins.

Don’t sweat the stuff you don’t understand immediately. Keep moving!

  • oCaU,
    • Zorn's Lemma in Agda

      Zorn’s lemma is one of the most fundamental theorems in classical mathematics. Yet homotopy type theory (HoTT), one of the frontiers of intuitionistic mathematics, has received relatively little research attention regarding its extension into the classical realm. This talk aims to help fill that gap. We present a proof of the classical theorem of Zorn’s lemma within the framework of HoTT, while introducing the relevant Agda syntax and basic concepts from HoTT along the way. (Zhihu link).

      Prerequisite: Agda
      Note: Depending on audience interest, the talk may also cover the relevant Agda syntax and basic concepts from HoTT.

    • Synthetic Gödel Incompleteness in Agda

      This is an exceptionally concise formalization of Gödel’s incompleteness theorem. We introduce the Church–Turing thesis (as an axiom) in HoTT and establish an environment for synthetic computability theory. This lets us reach the heart of the argument for Gödel’s incompleteness theorem directly, without delving into complicated models of computation or encoding details.

      Prerequisites: Agda + basic knowledge of mathematical logic.

  • 子鱼, Progress in LLM-Based Automated Theorem Proving

    A general-audience introduction to the latest methods and results in automated theorem proving driven by large language models.

    Prerequisites: Virtually none.
    Note: This talk is not closely related to type theory; think of it as an appetizer or dessert.

  • Trebor,
    • Foundations of Categorical Semantics

      Dependent type theory is the language of fibrations. Using this as the central thread, the talk develops the broad framework of categorical semantics from the ground up and explains how categorical semantics can be applied to solve problems in practice.

      Prerequisites: Familiarity with dependent type theory and the foundations of category theory.
      Note: This topic can be divided into multiple sessions—the more sessions, the greater the depth and the fewer prerequisites required.

    • An Overview of Synthetic Mathematics

      We are accustomed to representing every mathematical object in terms of more fundamental mathematical objects: encoding the real numbers with Cauchy sequences, using the real numbers to construct the plane, and then studying plane geometry. Euclid’s Elements, by contrast, directly uses axioms to characterize the objects under study, thereby constructing an exceptionally convenient language for geometry—much like a DSL in programming languages. This talk presents examples and applications of synthetic mathematics. nLab link

      Prerequisites: Familiarity with dependent type theory and the foundations of category theory.
      Note: One of the listed topics may be selected; if time permits, all of them may be covered.

    • The Effective Topos: A Bridge Between Constructivism and Computability (also known as: How to Compile Your Proof)

      Constructivism is often conflated with computable mathematics, yet many constructivist systems have no obvious computational interpretation. Meanwhile, conventional computability theory deals only with mappings between natural numbers, whereas mathematics often requires us to consider the computability of other kinds of objects. The effective topos provides a systematic language connecting constructivism and computability.

      Prerequisites: None. Some familiarity with basic definitions from logic and category theory (such as what a universally quantified proposition is and the definition of a category) would be helpful.
      Note: One of the listed topics may be selected; if time permits, all of them may be covered.

    • Pluralism in the Foundations of Mathematics

      Note: A general-audience talk.

  • 千里冰封 (abstracts for all talks),
    • The Not-So-Cool cooltt

      An introduction to the Lamett project, focusing on components selected according to community interest.

      Prerequisites: Familiarity with implementations of MLTT (see Mini-TT and elaboration-zoo). A basic knowledge of Java programming is also needed to understand the code.
      Note: If you would prefer to hear about something else, feel free to contact me directly.

    • Implementing Advanced Features in Type Theory

      A brief introduction to several advanced examples presented in elaboration-zoo (metavariable solving, implicit arguments, and more), as well as how to implement pattern matching.

      Prerequisite: A thorough understanding of the features being implemented.
      Slides

  • Gestellmensch, Set Theory Is Dead; Long Live Type Theory

    The mathematical background of HoTT and the univalence axiom: homotopy theory, moduli spaces, and many related topics—as well as why (homotopy) type theory provides a better foundation for mathematics.

    Prerequisites: Not sure; the talk may offer a broad, informal overview, mostly for fun.

  • Anqur, Words of Encouragement: What Can You Gain from Dependently Typed JS?

    An introduction to the RowScript project; how to extend a language from the most basic NbE; and a few words of encouragement.

    Prerequisite: Some intuition for dependent types would be helpful, but is not required.

  • 林子篆, WASI and the Component Model: Sharing Resources Across Languages

    WASI is an interface designed to let WebAssembly programs access operating-system resources, while the component model allows modules implemented in different languages to exchange data. How can linear types and effect systems prevent errors when resources are exchanged between modules, and what limitations remain in practice?

    Prerequisite: A general familiarity with immutable functional programming is sufficient.
    Note:

    • The methods introduced here are not guaranteed to enter the standard. People in the standards community are interested, but
    • The presentation will not be formal or mathematical. These models are prototypes, and I will not investigate them in greater depth until it is more certain that they will enter the standard.
  • 魔理沙, Partial eval, Staging, Compiler and NBE

    An introduction to partial evaluators, using them as a starting point to explain their connections with techniques such as staging, compilers, and normalization by evaluation.

    Prerequisite: Preferably an understanding of definitional interpreters.

  • 游客账户0x0, Implementing MLTT Step by Step

    An implementation of Per Martin-Löf type theory in OCaml.

Frequently Asked Questions

  1. Is this event online or in person?

    Online.

  2. Will this event be recorded?

    Recordings will be uploaded to Bilibili.

  3. Will materials related to the course content be provided?

    ntype-cafe-summer-school-2026 will be updated throughout the event with lecture notes, slides, code, and recommended supplementary materials.

  4. How can people outside China participate despite the time difference?

    We currently plan to hold sessions in the afternoons and evenings in the China time zone. Participants should arrange their schedules accordingly. If an unavoidable scheduling conflict arises, please see Q2 and Q3.

  5. Can exercises or assignments be provided after class?

    Certainly. We will discuss this with the lecturers. Follow-up Q&A sessions may also be arranged; for now, we tentatively plan to hold these in the QQ group.

  6. Can additional support be provided for beginners?

    This is something the organizers have consistently advocated. The original summer-school plan did not include prerequisite courses, but the organizers felt that this might exclude a large proportion of interested participants, so we prepared these foundation courses with everyone in mind. The survey results confirmed that this was necessary: only %10 of respondents were familiar with type theory.

  7. Can the event adequately accommodate participants from different fields?

    At present, students in mathematics and computer science each account for nearly half of the audience. We are doing our best to balance the relevance of the talks, and the talk list includes material from both fields.

  8. What kind of organization is Infinity Type Café?

    It is an organization whose members are mostly students. We are especially grateful to everyone who has offered us support and encouragement in their feedback!
