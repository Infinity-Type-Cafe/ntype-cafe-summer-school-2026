The ∞-type Café Summer School 2026 will officially begin on July 2, 2026. The event arose almost by chance from a simple suggestion: could we organize a summer program on type theory? There are currently very few scholars working on type theory in China, and relevant resources are equally scarce. So we decided to give it a try!

What is type theory? Born in logic, it grew up in mathematics, blossomed in computer science, and bore fruit in programming languages. It spans several closely intertwined disciplines. In mathematics, it has emerged as a new foundation for the subject, alongside set theory and category theory. In computer science, the word “type” immediately brings to mind the many kinds of programming languages in which types help compilers or interpreters perform compile-time checks. The emergence of the Curry–Howard correspondence prompted us to reconsider types, and the propositions-as-types perspective gave rise to type-based interactive proof assistants. It has become possible to write the proof of a concrete problem as a program—but to write such a program, one must first learn and become comfortable with the language it uses. On this point, students of both mathematics and computer science face their own difficulties. Mathematics students may first need to overcome the hurdle of programming, while computer science students may find the underlying mathematical foundations equally challenging. After overcoming countless difficulties, you finally write a program that a proof assistant accepts—but have you ever asked yourself whether it really is a proof? Are the functions defined in it well-defined? Are the objects constructed in it reasonable? Are the techniques it uses valid? Questions like these will keep pushing you forward, farther and farther...

One day, a beginner may happen upon type theory and begin exploring it. Their path will surely be full of twists and turns, because that is how we began as well. Through this event, we hope to help more students who want to study type theory, or simply learn what types are about. In return, we will also make more friends. That is our simple and sincere vision.

To quote 狗先生, the owner of the Infinity Type Café group:

   > On a cold night, two children lit a campfire, and more and more lost children gathered around it. They felt immeasurably happy.<br> 
   > When day broke, the children gradually found their own paths and left one by one. But they would never forget the campfire that had once kept them warm.

* **oCaU**,
    * <font color='#fa5252'><i>Zorn's Lemma in Agda</i></font>
        > Zorn's lemma is one of the most fundamental theorems in classical mathematics. Yet homotopy type theory (HoTT), one of the frontiers of intuitionistic mathematics, has received relatively little research attention regarding its extension into the classical realm. This talk aims to help fill that gap. We present a proof of the classical theorem of Zorn's lemma within the framework of HoTT, while introducing the relevant Agda syntax and basic concepts from HoTT along the way. ([Zhihu link](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/629641633)).<br>

        Prerequisite: Agda <br>
        Note: Depending on audience interest, the talk may also cover the relevant Agda syntax and basic concepts from HoTT.

    * <font color='#fa5252'><i>Synthetic Gödel Incompleteness in Agda</i></font>
        > This is an exceptionally concise formalization of Gödel's incompleteness theorem. We introduce the Church–Turing thesis (as an axiom) in HoTT and establish an environment for synthetic computability theory. This lets us reach the heart of the argument for Gödel's incompleteness theorem directly, without delving into complicated models of computation or encoding details.<br>

        Prerequisites: Agda + basic knowledge of mathematical logic. <br>

* **子鱼**, <font color='#fa5252'><i>Progress in LLM-Based Automated Theorem Proving</i></font>
    > A general-audience introduction to the latest methods and results in automated theorem proving driven by large language models.<br>

    Prerequisites: Virtually none.<br>
    Note: This talk is not closely related to type theory; think of it as an appetizer or dessert.

* **Trebor**,
    * <font color='#fa5252'><i>Foundations of Categorical Semantics</i></font>
        > Dependent type theory is the language of fibrations. Using this as the central thread, the talk develops the broad framework of categorical semantics from the ground up and explains how categorical semantics can be applied to solve problems in practice. <br>

        Prerequisites: Familiarity with dependent type theory and the foundations of category theory.<br>
        Note: This topic can be divided into multiple sessions—the more sessions, the greater the depth and the fewer prerequisites required.

    * <font color='#fa5252'><i>An Overview of Synthetic Mathematics</i></font>
        > We are accustomed to representing every mathematical object in terms of more fundamental mathematical objects: encoding the real numbers with Cauchy sequences, using the real numbers to construct the plane, and then studying plane geometry. Euclid's *Elements*, by contrast, directly uses axioms to characterize the objects under study, thereby constructing an exceptionally convenient language for geometry—much like a DSL in programming languages. This talk presents examples and applications of synthetic mathematics. [nLab link](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/synthetic+mathematics) <br>

        Prerequisites: Familiarity with dependent type theory and the foundations of category theory.<br>
        Note: One of the listed topics may be selected; if time permits, all of them may be covered.

    * <font color='#fa5252'><i>The Effective Topos: A Bridge Between Constructivism and Computability (also known as: How to Compile Your Proof)</i></font>
        > Constructivism is often conflated with computable mathematics, yet many constructivist systems have no obvious computational interpretation. Meanwhile, conventional computability theory deals only with mappings between natural numbers, whereas mathematics often requires us to consider the computability of other kinds of objects. The effective topos provides a systematic language connecting constructivism and computability.<br>

        Prerequisites: None. Some familiarity with basic definitions from logic and category theory (such as what a universally quantified proposition is and the definition of a category) would be helpful.<br>
        Note: One of the listed topics may be selected; if time permits, all of them may be covered.

* **Gestellmensch**, <font color='#fa5252'><i>Set Theory Is Dead; Long Live Type Theory</i></font>
